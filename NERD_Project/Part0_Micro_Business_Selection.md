## 2. Niche Justification (top choice — Stewart Surfboards)

Stewart Surfboards is niche because it doesn't try to be a general sporting-goods
store — it hand-shapes custom surfboards (it's especially known for longboards)
for a narrow, dedicated group of surfers, which means its prices are driven by
craftsmanship and reputation rather than by competing on volume. That tight
focus is exactly what makes it a good fit for analysis: with one specialized
product line, I can cleanly isolate *why* customers pay a premium instead of
buying a cheaper mass-produced board.

---

## 3. Focus Question

> **Why can Stewart charge two-to-three times the price of a mass-produced,
> imported surfboard — and how much of that premium comes from the custom
> craftsmanship itself versus the value of the Stewart brand name?**

Why it's a good microeconomics question: it's specific, it's answerable from
public pricing, and it goes straight at core micro ideas — **product
differentiation**, **willingness to pay**, and **brand as a source of pricing
power** — rather than just describing the shop. It sets up the rest of the
project nicely: I can compare Stewart's price list against big-box/import boards,
look at what features command a markup, and end with a recommendation about where
its pricing power is strongest (or most at risk).
