## 3. Primary Justification — why Vietnam

I almost defaulted to a big, obvious economy, but the point of this project is to
understand an economy, not just describe a famous one — and Vietnam is the rare
country where the core drama of development economics is playing out right now.
Here are three reasons I picked Vietnam:

1. **It's a clean natural experiment in export-led growth.** Vietnam grew by
   about 7% in 2024 even though a lot of the world slowed down. The main thing
   driving it seems to be its manufacturing sector as firms started to look for
   ways to diversify their supply chains outside of China (I've seen this talked
   about as the "China + 1" strategy). That seems like a clear signal to look at
   for Part 1.
2. **The data seems like it is genuinely good!** I was able to find all of the
   data for GDP, inflation, trade, and sector breakdowns on the World Bank and
   Trading Economics with the most recent years completely filled in.
3. **The standard of living story is very interesting.** Vietnam went from being
   one of the world's poorest countries in the 1980s to a solid middle-income
   economy today, which makes the Part 2 work (things like the Big Mac Index,
   Dollar Street) make a lot more sense, as the average household has grown and
   changed a lot, even in the last ten years.

Poland and Chile are fairly strong backups, but I think these are both more
"finished" stories instead of Vietnam, which is one where I genuinely don't know
where the plot of the story is going to go next, and that's why I'm so excited to
explore it more.

## 4. Focus Question

> **Can Vietnam avoid the middle-income trap?**

As wages rise and its cheap-labor advantage fades, can it move up the value chain
from building other countries' products to designing and branding its own, and
can it do this fast enough to maintain its momentum and grow at 6–7% a year?

Or will its growth run out of energy and stall?

It's specific, researchable with public data that is pretty easy to find, and I
think it will end up proving interesting throughout the later parts of the project
too, like looking at what goes into its GDP composition and how it responds to
economic shocks (Part 1), rising wages and living standards (Part 2), the State
Bank of Vietnam's currency and industrial policy (Part 3), and a "one reform that
decides the outcome" macro pitch (Part 4).
